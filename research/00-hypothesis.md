## Summary

The speaker describes a batch process. Hydrogen sulfide from beneath the Giza plateau is converted to sulfur dioxide in the three small pyramids beside the Great Pyramid and fed through a floor shaft into the King's Chamber. Separately, water from an external reservoir and from an aquifer is driven by a reciprocating pump in the descending passage, through one-way valves in the Subterranean Chamber and up the well shaft, filling the Queen's Chamber and Grand Gallery in four strokes. Draining the Grand Gallery then acts as a *syringe*: it sucks outside air down the King's Chamber shafts and pulls air plus SO2 through four grooves into the granite antechamber, where lightning-induced electric fields are said to make the granite emit ultrasound that catalyses SO2 to SO3. Refilling the gallery in four strokes absorbs the gases as dilute sulfuric acid, which is withdrawn through the Queen's Chamber and sent on to the central (Khafre) pyramid and to rock-cut pits used as electrolyte switches.

The account is specific about sequence and topology and almost entirely unspecific about magnitudes: no volume, pressure, flow rate, temperature, concentration, field strength, frequency or cycle time is given anywhere in the three transcripts. The pump mechanism itself is said to be the same as in other pyramids discussed earlier in the recording, which is not in the supplied transcripts.

### The wider network the speaker places this in

Step Pyramid: extracts methane from bedrock. Serapeum: converts air into hydrogen and oxygen (as transcribed). Red Pyramid: hydrogen plus nitrogen to ammonia. Bent Pyramid: aqueous ammonia plus CO2 by-product to urea, via ammonium bicarbonate or carbonate, or alternatively nitric acid; its satellite pyramid is a hydraulic press working stone valves. Great Pyramid: sulfur and air to SO3 and dilute sulfuric acid. Central pyramid: sulfuric to hydrochloric acid. (F 00:03:19-00:06:10; 00:12:20-00:13:30; 00:27:19-00:28:12.) Pump shafts fed from a reservoir are said to work identically in the Step, Red and Bent pyramids (F 00:04:50-00:05:47; 00:41:15).

## 7. What a simulation of this domain needs

This domain is the specification itself, so the "simulation" is a staged model whose stages mirror section 1. Constitutive data and numbers must come from the other domain files; none are supplied here.

**Governing physics per stage (model classes, to be parameterised elsewhere)**

| Stage | Steps | Model class |
|---|---|---|
| Fill and pump | S08-S12, S18 | Hydrostatics for the reservoir-level fill; unsteady incompressible free-surface flow (two-phase Navier-Stokes or a 1-D pipe-network model with check valves and a moving piston boundary); seepage through masonry and bedrock (Darcy) as a loss term |
| Syringe draw | S13-S15, S17 | Falling free surface coupled to compressible gas flow through a network: air shafts, King's Chamber, grooves, antechamber, gallery; species transport for SO2, O2, N2 |
| Field and sound | S04, S06, S07 | Quasi-static and transient Maxwell equations in lossy dielectrics for ground-current and lightning excitation; linear piezoelectric constitutive coupling in an aggregate of quartz grains; linear acoustics for rock-to-air radiation and cavity resonance |
| Conversion | S16 | Gas-phase kinetics of SO2 oxidation with and without an acoustic term; plug-flow residence time in the antechamber |
| Absorption | S19-S22 | Gas-liquid mass transfer with reaction (film or penetration theory) at a rising free surface; aqueous S(IV) to S(VI) oxidation kinetics; heat of solution and reaction in the energy equation |
| Wall chemistry | S23 | Heterogeneous dissolution of calcium carbonate with a growing product layer (shrinking-core or passivation model) |
| Heat | S24 | Transient conduction through masonry between gallery and void; lumped energy balance on the batch |
| Network | S27-S29 | Lumped circuit of vein resistances with electrolyte-bridged gaps; simple stoichiometric mass balance for the salt reaction |

**Boundary and initial conditions the hypothesis fixes**

- Topology: the connections in C1-C10 and S01-S31. In particular the gallery communicates with the King's Chamber only through four grooves, and the King's Chamber with the atmosphere only through two shafts and with the gas supply through one floor opening.
- Initial state for the draw: gallery full of water, King's Chamber full of SO2.
- Initial well-shaft level: equal to the reservoir level.
- Excitation: slow ground current from below, impulsive lightning at a flat top.
- Everything else in section 5 is free and must be swept, bounded from independent sources, or back-solved as "what value would be required".

**Key dimensionless groups to report**

Reynolds and Froude numbers for passage and free-surface flow; Bond or Weber number where the free surface meets narrow slots; ratio of gas displaced to gas drawn through each path (a flow-split fraction); Damkohler number for antechamber conversion (residence time over reaction time); Hatta number and an absorption Damkohler number for S19-S22 (uptake rate over displacement rate, which decides claim S22); Peclet number for gas mixing in the gallery; Biot and Fourier numbers for wall heat loss; adiabatic temperature rise over boiling margin; electrical size (dimension over wavelength), dielectric relaxation time over step duration, and effective electromechanical coupling factor of the granite; resistance ratio of a filled to an empty pit.

**Pass/fail criteria, phrased from the speaker's own requirements**

Each is a yes/no test with the required value reported alongside, so the result can go either way.

1. *Reach:* can a reservoir-level fill plus a pump in the descending passage raise water to the top of the gallery at all, and what head and force does that need? (S09-S12)
2. *Four strokes:* is the swept volume of the available pump passage at least a quarter of the volume to be filled? (Q01)
3. *Containment:* do the gallery and Queen's Chamber hold that water column long enough for one cycle, given measured or bounded masonry leakage? (item 11 of section 5)
4. *Syringe:* when the gallery level falls, what fractions of the make-up gas arrive via the air shafts, via the grooves, and via other leaks; is the flow through the grooves non-trivial? (S13-S15)
5. *Gas retention:* does SO2 remain in the King's Chamber between delivery and draw with the air shafts open? (S03)
6. *Field:* what field does a realistic lightning or ground-current input produce at the granite, and for how long? (S06)
7. *Sound:* what acoustic pressure does that field produce in the antechamber air by the piezoelectric route? (S07)
8. *Catalysis:* at that acoustic level and residence time, what fraction of SO2 becomes SO3, compared with no sound at all? (S16)
9. *No push-back:* does uptake keep pace with the rising water so that no gas is forced back through the grooves? (S22)
10. *Thermal:* does four-stage filling keep the batch below boiling for the stated chemistry? (S21)
11. *Self-protection:* does a calcium sulfate layer form and limit wall loss to a rate compatible with the surviving surfaces? (S23)
12. *Mass balance:* for a plausible H2S flux, what mass of acid per cycle and per year results? (S01-S26)
13. *Switching:* does filling a pit with dilute acid change the vein-to-vein resistance enough to act as a switch? (S28)

A result should be reported as "feasible with required parameter X = value", "infeasible for all X in the physically bounded range", or "undetermined because the hypothesis does not define X".
