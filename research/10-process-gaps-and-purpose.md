# 10. What the claimed process still needs, and what the evidence says the structure was for

Date: 2026-10-10. Status: synthesis of files 00 to 09 and the Tier 0 screening. No new source was read for this file.

## 1. Scope and method

The project owner asked two questions:

1. What additional elements would the claimed process need before it could be called a feasible process with a reasonable objective, and what does scientific evidence say about each?
2. What does the evidence support about the purpose of the structure, without conjecture?

This file answers from the evidence base only: `00-hypothesis.md` (the claim as stated), `01` to `07` (one file per domain, each closed by a verification pass whose corrections override earlier text), `09-recomputation.md` (every derived figure recomputed, conflicts resolved) and the committed screening results in `results/tier0/`. For each domain one reader extracted the requirements and a second reader checked every item against the files, removing what the files do not state and softening any status the files do not support, in either direction.

### Status words

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| evidenced | The files hold positive evidence that the element exists or works at the needed magnitude. |
| not evidenced | A search found no evidence that the element exists. This is absence of evidence, not a contradiction. |
| contradicted by measurement | A survey or measurement in the files disagrees with the element as claimed. |
| physically excluded | A calculation from sourced values rules the element out as claimed. The scope of each exclusion is stated. |
| unknown | The deciding input is not specified by the hypothesis, or not measured, so the files cannot decide. |

Where a magnitude rests on a derived estimate, an assumed input, an abstract or a value recalled from memory, the item says so. Screening verdicts (`pass`, `fail`, `conditional`, `undefined`, `pending`) are quoted from `results/tier0/tier0.json` at ledger digest `b5b35856`.

## 2. The first gap: there is no design basis

A process is judged against an objective: what it makes, how strong, how much, how often, from what feed. The hypothesis as stated in the source videos defines none of these. `09-recomputation.md` (section 6, item 12) lists the inputs that no recomputation can supply because the claim does not contain them: the pump's stroke length, which spaces fill with water, the sulfur dioxide fraction of the chamber gas and its source rate, the drain time, the ultrasound frequency and intensity, and the acid strength in the pits. `00-hypothesis.md` (section 5) lists about thirty such inputs.

The consequence is that several steps cannot fail or pass; they are `undefined` or `conditional`. A feasible process objective would have to state at least:

| Design quantity | What the hypothesis says | What the files can bound |
|---|---|---|
| Product strength | "dilute" | At most 0.023 mol/L (0.23 percent by weight, pH about 1.5) per batch if sulfur-limited, 0.012 mol/L if the oxygen comes with the same draw of air (derived; assumes a King's Chamber of pure sulfur dioxide at 1 atm, complete capture and oxidation, no wall loss) |
| Product quantity | not stated | At most 1.3 t of sulfuric acid per batch under the same assumptions (derived) |
| Cycle time, cycles per year | not stated | Not bounded. Screening takes one day as a judgement call |
| Feed rate and composition | not stated | No hydrogen sulfide flux or concentration has been measured at Giza |
| Strength the stated uses need | not stated | Not sourced in the files for leaching or metal separation |
| Water level of the source | "external reservoir" | Old Kingdom flood peak 43 to 46 m below the pyramid's base |

Until the product strength and tonnage required by a named use are set beside the ceiling the structure could deliver, "feasible" has no test. The chemistry file's own thresholds (for example a product of at least 0.05 mol/L) are labelled judgement calls; the ceiling of 0.012 to 0.023 mol/L lies below that pass line at any gas fraction (screening verdict S30: `conditional`).

Evidence, for and against:

- **Sulfur source.** One peer-reviewed paper, read in full (Badawy 2021), infers from mineralogy at one exposure that hydrogen sulfide once drove sulfuric-acid cave formation on the plateau. It measured no gas, water, isotope or age, reports no present seepage and calls the gas probably bacterial. The exposure is in the Wadi Garawi Formation at Darb El Fayum, not in the Mokattam beds under the pyramids. Cretaceous carbonates lie 25 to 150 m below the Sphinx and Khafre area (abstract only) and are imaged as saturated with saline water. Sulfidic springs exist at Helwan, about 24 km away, with no concentration given (`07`, section 3 and verification checks 1, 2, 13, 15).
- **The patent.** US 9,108,846 was re-read at source. Its three steps are the whole route to acid: hydrogen sulfide to sulfur dioxide, a vanadium catalyst at 390 to 430 °C for the trioxide, and condensation at 60 to 120 °C. The speaker describes the patent correctly; no step in it runs at ambient temperature without a catalyst (`03`, table 2c, check V14).
- **The floor.** Petrie records the floor dug away under the west end, below the coffer. No source opened describes an original lined shaft there. The speaker himself calls the link to the satellite pyramids a hypothesis (`01`, claim 9).
- **The grooves.** Petrie measured a groove depth of 2.8 in at 8 to 10 in above the doorway, falling to 1.75 to 2.0 in at about 5 in above it, which runs out within half an inch of the door top; the residual area is at most 0.002 m2 (derived). His conclusion is hedged ("probably ended"): the last 5 in are broken away and only two of the four grooves could be measured. A slab clearance of about 1.5 cm would pass about 0.02 m2 per slab edge (the geometry file's arithmetic from one reported fragment), so gas could pass the slabs by another path than the one claimed (`01`, claim 2, check 1).
- **In the hypothesis's favour.** The two King's Chamber shafts can admit the air the draw needs at about 25 Pa for a one-hour drain (derived, friction factor assumed). Slots for three portcullis slabs exist. Sulfur dioxide is very soluble (1.3 mol/L/atm at 298 K, read at source): a Gallery of water takes up at least 99 percent of the largest charge at equilibrium, though the rate of uptake across metres of still gas is untested.

## 4. Oxidation: gas phase

| Element | Needed | Status | What would close the gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sulfur dioxide to trioxide in the gas at ambient temperature, with ultrasound as the catalyst (S16) | A first-order rate near 2e-4 per second for a one-hour half-life | physically excluded, for the direct gas route | A hot solid catalyst (industry: vanadium pentoxide near 430 °C), a nitrogen-oxide carrier at process level, or a demonstrated ultrasonic effect worth 13 orders of magnitude |
| Lightning as the source of nitrogen oxides for a lead-chamber route (not claimed; tested as the strongest alternative) | About 100 mol of nitrogen per batch of 1.33e4 mol sulfur (derived) | not evidenced at process level; lightning as the source falls four to five orders short on an assumed 100 ppb of nitrogen oxides, which the file calls generous | A concentrated nitrate or nitric acid source at the pyramid giving a nitrogen-oxide fraction of about 1e-4 or more. That would be an addition to the hypothesis |
| Hydroxyl radicals, or iron-oxide and dust surfaces (not claimed; tested) | Of order 1e4 mol of radicals per batch, or a surface that turns over | physically excluded | A dark radical source of that size, or a surface that does not saturate |

Evidence:

- Thermodynamics allows the reaction: it releases 98.9 kJ/mol and its equilibrium constant is 2.05e12 bar^-1/2 at 300 K. The obstacle is the rate. The uncatalysed rate constant is at most 7e-37 cm3 per molecule per second at 300 K, a lifetime of at least 8.8e9 years, 13.7 orders of magnitude short of a one-hour half-life (`03`, C3, checks V1 and V2; `09`, rows 29, 30). Screening verdict S16: `fail`, a converted fraction of 9e-14 at most in a pass of 10 minutes to 6 hours.
- Cavitation, the mechanism of sonochemistry, occurs only in liquids. Acoustic heating of the gas speeds the reaction 1.5 to 2.2 times at 172 dB, the strongest airborne ultrasound engineered, while the granite source is estimated at 76 dB (screening verdicts E18 `conditional`, S07 `fail`).
- Ultrasound is documented to speed gas-solid catalysis in two settings, each with a working catalyst at reaction temperature and an engineered source, neither with sulfur dioxide. A surface-mediated variant is therefore unsupported here, not excluded by mechanism (`05`, claim C1 and its verification note).
- A low-temperature route did run industrially: the lead-chamber process made acid of 60 to 70 percent by weight with nitrogen oxides carrying the oxygen. One Gallery stroke of air at 100 ppb nitrogen oxides (assumed, and called generous) carries 2.4e-3 mol, four to five orders short of the make-up needed (`03`, checks V10 and V12).

## 5. Oxidation in the water, and the oxygen it needs

With no trioxide formed in the gas, all acid must come from dissolved sulfur dioxide oxidised in the water (S19, S20).

| Element | Needed | Status | What would close the gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| A dissolved iron or manganese catalyst | Iron(III) near 1e-5 mol/L or manganese(II) near 1e-4 mol/L | unknown | Measured levels in the feed water, or leached from Mokattam limestone and mortar at pH 1.5 to 2; the rate laws transcribed cleanly with their limits |
| Oxygen crossing into the water | About 6.6e3 mol per largest batch. The fill water holds 158 mol (2.4 percent) | unknown; excluded only for a still, unsparged surface | A sourced air-water transfer coefficient for the Gallery, and an aeration step (cascading, bubbling) raising transfer 1e3 to 1e4 times above a still surface |
| Enough air in the draw | About 760 m3 of air for a full chamber charge | not met by one stroke of 573 m3 | A stated gas fraction and number of draws |
| Sulfur dioxide not lost to damp limestone first | Not sized | unknown | A measured deposition velocity onto damp limestone in still air |

Evidence:

- Catalysis by iron and manganese ions is well established. At 1e-5 mol/L iron(III) the oxidation takes 19 h by one rate law and 0.2 to 3 h by another; at 1e-4 mol/L manganese(II) about 5 h. These laws are used two to three orders above the sulfur concentrations they were tested at, and the iron and manganese levels at Giza are unmeasured. The chemistry file calls this the strongest point in the hypothesis's favour: if those levels are present, chemistry is not the limit (`03`, C5, checks V6 and V16).
- Oxygen is the limit. Through a still surface of 25 to 27.6 m2 the oxygen for one batch takes 2.7 to 30 years to cross (transfer coefficient assumed, unsourced); an agitated surface brings this to about 100 days (`09`, row 33). The file rates the claim under vigorous aeration as untested, not contradicted. No aeration step is described.
- Screening verdict S20: `fail`. In one day the dissolved oxygen (158.1 mol) and the oxygen crossing the still surface (6.6 mol) can oxidise 2.5 percent of a full batch (2.1 to 2.8 percent over the input ranges). This word rests on reading "the King's Chamber full of sulfur dioxide" (S13) as pure gas; the hypothesis states no gas fraction, and with a batch of 55 percent of a chamber or less the favourable end clears the 5 percent line. The figure that does not depend on that reading: one day's oxygen is about 165 mol, which can make at most about 330 mol of acid, 0.0006 mol/L in the Gallery's 573 m3 (arithmetic on the committed result).

## 6. Product, heat, walls and the hydrogen chloride step

| Element | Needed | Status | What would close the gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| A product strong enough for its stated uses (S30) | Not defined on either side | unknown | A sourced minimum strength and tonnage for leaching or metal separation; a stated gas fraction, water volume and cycle |
| Heat violent enough to need staged filling and a heat exchanger (S21, S24) | About 1.0 mol/L made in place, 44 times the ceiling | physically excluded, at the ceiling strength | About 44 times more sulfur dioxide than the King's Chamber holds at 1 atm |
| Acid surviving limestone walls and gypsum mortar (S23) | A gypsum coat that forms and lasts through fill and drain cycles | unknown | Walls coated beforehand by weeks to months of acid; a coat shown to survive each fresh-water fill; mortar joints shown to hold |
| Hydrogen chloride gas from dilute acid and salt at ambient temperature (S27) | A partial pressure near 1e-3 atm, which needs more than 22 percent hydrochloric acid by weight | physically excluded | Acid concentrated far above the ceiling together with strong heating, or distillation. Neither is described |

Evidence:

- **Heat.** The largest batch releases 4.34e9 J, about 1200 kWh, and warms 573 m3 of water by 1.7 to 1.8 K; if the first of four fills took it all, 7.3 K (screening verdicts S21 `pass`, nothing boils over; S24 `fail`, no heat exchanger is needed). Industry avoids absorbing trioxide in water because of acid mist, not boiling (`03`, checks V4 and V8).
- **Walls.** Gypsum armouring is real: calcite columns fed pH 2 solutions with sulfate became inert after 122 to 7030 h, and mine water at pH 2.9 armoured limestone in 48 h. Those feeds carried 250 to 1500 ppm iron(III) under constant flow. Gypsum is sparingly soluble, not insoluble, one study finds attack decaying but never reaching zero, and the file expects a fresh-water fill to strip the coat (`03`, C10, checks V9 and V17). Screening verdict E29: `conditional`; bare limestone neutralises 41 percent of the strongest batch (32 to 77 percent) before gypsum saturates.
- **Hydrogen chloride.** Over the dilute acid and salt the partial pressure is about 1.2e-7 atm (derived; activity coefficients recalled, and tabulated data agree: 1.1e-7 atm over 2 percent acid at 25 °C). Screening verdict S27: `fail`. Khafre's boat pits are rich in halite (chlorine up to 26.7 percent by weight, in a report that is not peer reviewed), but halite with gypsum is this limestone's normal salt assemblage (`07`, C11 and C13).

| A route, driver and destination for the liquid leaving through the Queen's Chamber (S26) | An outlet, a driver and a receiving point | not evidenced; not stated by the hypothesis | The hypothesis naming the route first |

Evidence:

- **Levels.** The pyramid's base is about 60 m above sea level (plus or minus 2 m; no surveyed pavement benchmark). The Old Kingdom flood peak is modelled at 13.5 to 14 m (Lehner) or about 17 m (Younes), 43 to 46 m below the base. Navigable Nile water reached the plateau foot and rain reaches the plateau. After a specific search, no dam, basin, lining or lake sediment is reported at or above Khufu's pavement, and the documented drainage runs south-east, away from Khufu, over karstified limestone. One 50 mm storm at 30 percent runoff over 0.06 km2 would yield one Gallery fill (the file's arithmetic, with a catchment and basin that are not evidenced). Old Kingdom builders could impound runoff (the 14 m Sadd el-Kafara dam), and peer-reviewed but unexcavated hydraulic proposals exist for Saqqara and Dahshur; the hydraulics file judges that the Saqqara argument does not transfer to Giza (`02`, C1, section 2.2, checks 1 to 5, Steelman 1 to 6).
- **Head and work.** The head from the passage end (-30.0 m) to the Gallery roof (+51.5 m) is 81.5 m, 0.80 MPa. The ideal work per fill is 260 MJ from pavement level (about 72 kWh, roughly 960 person-hours at an unsourced 75 W) and 641 to 651 MJ from the flood level. Energy is not the obstacle; a head source is. No assessment exists of whether the lining blocks stay seated under 0.3 to 0.8 MPa (`02`, sections 4.1 and 4.3; `09`, rows 17 and 21). Screening verdict S09: `conditional` on the reservoir; S10: `undefined`, no actuator or seal is named.
- **Strokes.** The Gallery (573 m3) over the full Descending Passage (133 m3) gives 4.3 strokes, within 10 percent of the stated four under the most generous reading, and the figure survives recomputation. With the Queen's Chamber, its passage, the Ascending Passage and the well shaft filling too, the volume is about 868 m3 and four full strokes deliver 533 m3: 6.5 strokes over the full passage, 8.9 below the Ascending junction, 10.2 below the pavement (`02`, C6; `09`, rows 1, 10, 15, 20). Screening verdicts S12, S18: `conditional`, the hypothesis fixes no stroke.
- **The pump.** No piston, rail, seal, rope wear or actuator is described in any source opened. The speaker says the mechanism matches pumps in other pyramids, in a part of the recording that is not transcribed. A granite plug on stone (friction 0.5, assumed) does not slide; even without friction, 1.01 MN of weight needs about 69 m of granite in a 105 m passage. A 1 mm gap at 0.75 MPa leaks up to 0.28 m3/s (`02`, C5, sections 4.2 and 4.6; `09`, rows 18, 19, 24).
- **Valves.** Petrie reports no valve, seat, hinge socket or fitting in the pit or chamber. The speaker cites a drawing by an unnamed German team, which could not be identified, and Cadman's small model, reported second-hand to pulse 60 to 80 times a minute with no pressure, flow, lift or scale published (`02`, C3, C13). Screening verdict E14 `fail`: a hydraulic ram at the passage foot would spill 2022 m3 (1259 at the least) into a 300 m3 chamber with no outfall.
- **Suction.** The suction limit is 10.1 m (6 to 8 m in practice). The lift from Old Kingdom levels is 13 to 23 m to the passage end; break-even needs a water table at 19.9 m above sea level (22 to 24 m in practice). Before 1837 the pit ended about 34.6 m below the base; Perring cut 11.0 to 11.6 m further through solid rock and the passages read mention no water. Plateau shaft water in 1837 to 1838 stood 44.6 to 47.3 m below the base and did not rise with the flood. An aquifer underlies the plateau today, and perched water is interpreted (not drilled) at 22 to 48 m near Menkaure; none is reported at Khufu, and no water-table value under Khufu exists for any period, so the exclusion is conditional on that absence (`02`, C7, C8, W1, W2; `09`, row 22). Screening verdicts S11 `fail` (margin -5.9 m at the flood peak, -0.4 m at the most favourable documented level); E17 `fail` (the pit as it stood before 1837 cleared the flood peak by 11.4 m, while the 1837 shaft comes within 2.9 m of today's water table, so modern groundwater cannot be excluded as the cause of the wet fill the speaker saw).
- **Tightness.** Backing limestone has 21 to 33 percent porosity and 12 to 20 percent absorption (fallen fragments from all three pyramids); about 96 percent of pore volume in five Khufu specimens is 0.02 to 0.23 micrometre, the signature of a tight matrix, so joints decide the leak-off. The cubic-law loss of a 570 m3 Gallery is about 5 minutes at 0.5 mm open joints, 9.5 hours at 0.1 mm, 3 days at 0.05 mm. No permeability is measured for Giza stone or mortar and no interior joint aperture is recorded; the claim that the limestone is impermeable is contradicted as an absolute (`02`, section 2.3, C12, section 4.6).
- **Air and gas paths.** The two King's Chamber shafts (about 0.05 m2 each, about 71 and 53 m long) pass a one-hour drain at about 25 Pa; Vyse's party recorded air rushing in when the southern channel was cleared in 1837. Petrie found both blocked in 1881, and the casing is gone, so the original exits are unknown (`02`, C9, section 4.5; `01`, claim 1). For the antechamber: three pairs of portcullis slots and drilled granite fragments show that slabs very probably existed, but grooves that pass gas pass water, so a full Gallery either traps a roof gas pocket, floods the King's Chamber, or needs about 84 kPa gauge that open shafts would vent (`01`, claim 2; `02`, C11). The Queen's Chamber connects only to its passage; its shafts were left uncut 13 cm short of the chamber until 1872 (`01`, claims 3 and 11).

Evidence:

- **Sources.** At 0.1 V/km (a storm-time low-latitude value seen in a snippet) the telluric potential across the 230 m base is 23 mV; at the 20 V/km hundred-year high-latitude extreme, 4.6 V, and about a tenth of that at Giza. Telluric variations are millihertz to hertz, horizontal and alternating; at those frequencies the pyramid is a resistive body with no resonance (`04`, C3, C4, section 4.5). Egypt has Africa's lowest national flash density, 0.2 flashes per km2 per year; direct strikes on the pyramid come at 0.03 to 0.12 per year, about 0.04 per year at a one-third ground share, each about 100 microseconds long (`04`, checks V8, V9, V16; `09`, row 41). An 1859 Siemens account of summit sparks in a dust storm exists only in secondary retellings, and blowing-sand electrification does not depend on the building's design.
- **The 2018 paper.** It is real. It models plane waves of 200 to 600 m wavelength (0.5 to 1.5 MHz) on a complete pointed pyramid of permittivity 5 + 0.1i. For the wave from above the model King's Chamber intensity is the higher one below about 270 m (a peak ratio of about 35 in free space, 3.2 to 4.6 with a ground); for the wave from the base side the authors' text says the field concentrates in the apex region, matching the speaker's description. The paper contains no lightning, telluric current, charge, capstone, truncation, granite or piezoelectricity, and the from-below case is free space only. Two in-situ datasets put the rock's loss two to three and a half orders above the model's (derived), so a resonance of the real structure is untested and doubtful. The speaker himself says the model used radio waves and would need rerunning with voltage inputs (`04`, C1, C2, checks V1 to V6; `00`, section 6 item 13). Screening verdicts E19, E20: `pending`, the solver plan.
- **Storage.** A capacitor needs an insulating dielectric. The outer 2 m of the pyramid's limestone measures 300 to 1500 ohm m, so a stored field relaxes in 4e-9 to 9e-8 s; Aswan granite in 5e-7 to 2e-2 s; storm fields evolve over seconds (`04`, C5, section 4.3). Screening verdict E22: `fail`.
- **Piezoelectricity.** Quartz is piezoelectric and granite shows a small measured bulk response, 1.4e-15 C/N (direct effect, one laboratory piece); an atomic-force-microscope study reports 7.0e-13 C/N at grain scale with the probe location not given. Single-crystal quartz is 2.3e-12 C/N. Granites tested never exceeded the statistical residue of randomly oriented grains, and no measurement on Aswan granite was found (`04`, C6, checks V10 to V12, V15).
- **Acoustic output.** A 1 kV/m lightning pulse yields 1.6e-15 to 4.1e-10 W/cm2 in the gas; a sustained 40 kHz field at the air-breakdown limit with the bulk coefficient yields 3.8e-9 W/cm2 (76 dB), 7 to 8 orders below the weakest documented gas-solid effect (0.30 W/cm2, 155 dB) and 10 to 11 orders below the 189 dB needed to cavitate a wet film. With the grain-scale coefficient a sustained 8e5 V/m drive on a block resonance with an assumed Q of 100 would reach 1 W/cm2 in air; no natural source of such a drive is identified. The best engineered airborne sources reach 172 dB (`04`, section 4.6, check V13; `05`, sections 4.1 to 4.4; `09`, rows 43, 46, 47). Screening verdict S07: `fail`, 76 dB, and 158 dB with every input at its generous end, against 190 dB.
- **The room.** A passive room adds no energy; the derived gain over the free-field level at 1 m is +3.3 dB at 40 kHz and +7.1 dB at 20 kHz, with air absorption (1.3 dB/m at 40 kHz) dominating. Holding 160 dB at 40 kHz would need about 57 kW. No acoustic measurement of the antechamber was found, so the claim that it has the most striking acoustics in the pyramid has no evidence (`05`, C5, C6, section 4.3). King's Chamber reports near 30 Hz match computed modes, weak support that the rooms behave as ordinary hard-walled cavities.
- **Mechanism.** Sonochemistry works through cavitation, which occurs only in liquids; no homogeneous gas-phase case was found. At 172 dB sound carries 1.1e-4 eV per molecule (0.4 percent of kT) and swings the temperature 9.3 K, a rate factor of 1.47 for an assumed 100 kJ/mol barrier; doubling an Arrhenius rate would need about 175 dB (`05`, C1, section 4.1; `09`, row 48). Screening verdict E18: `conditional`, a gain of 1.4 to 2.3 at 172 dB depending on the barrier. Ultrasound does speed two documented gas-solid reactions (up to 160 percent on a packed cracking bed near 1 W/cm2 at 340 to 570 °C; four- to sixfold CO oxidation on platinum over lithium niobate), each with a working catalyst and an engineered source, neither with sulfur dioxide or any rock (`05`, section 2.5). Sonochemistry does oxidise dissolved sulfite, in water, at 0.4 W/cm2 and above.
- **The flow meter.** The south shaft's slab (63.6 m, 60 mm thick, judged Tura limestone with two pins judged copper by colour) and the similar north door are real. Through-path loss to the north shaft is 101 to 119 dB at 13 kHz against a source of 50 to 60 dB; at 40 kHz the loss is 236 to 254 dB. Tura stone is 96 to 99 percent calcite with 0.5 to 2.5 percent quartz (one dataset). The speaker labels the device speculative (`05`, C8 to C14; `04`, C7; `09`, row 50). Screening verdict S25: `fail`, -51 dB at the receiver.

Evidence:

- **Veins.** Red and black iron and manganese oxide fills of fractures and karst are real and acknowledged by every side. Coarse specular hematite measures 0.003 to 7 ohm m dry (the best value is one Brazilian sample), a contrast of up to 3e4 over 100 ohm m rock, enough to carry current some 30 m. Earthy red hematite measures 7.2e4 to 3.5e6 ohm m, above the host. Observers describe the Giza fills as red, ochreous or dull black; none is described as specularite, and no Giza fill has been measured. A damp, halite-laden fill might conduct through brine at a few tenths of an ohm m (the file's own unsourced estimate), so the veins should not be called insulators without a measurement either (`07`, section 4.2, C11; `09`, row 44). Screening verdicts S28, S29, E38: `conditional`.
- **Network.** No published vein map exists; the basis is the speaker's own map from about 12 visits. One transient-electromagnetic survey images linear conductive zones trending south-west to north-east in the Sphinx and Khafre area (abstract only); they are read as water-saturated faults on the natural joint set. The stated age of the veins (hundreds of millions of years) is contradicted: the host rock is at most 48.07 million years old (`07`, C4 and C5).
- **Current.** Lightning glass in fractures metres deep and lightning-made microspherules are documented elsewhere, in granitic rock (abstracts), so the fulgurite claim is testable at Giza by ordinary microanalysis. No fulgurite, vitrified-rock analysis or microspherule from Giza is documented; the article cited for them never uses the words fulgurite, spherule or sample, and its authors call the veins natural scars that predate the pyramids (`07`, C7 to C10). Screening verdict E22 `fail`: charge in the granite relaxes in 5.3 ms (21 ms at most) and in the limestone in 31 ns, against a storm field that changes over seconds.
- **Boat pits.** The ISIDA web report (not peer reviewed; 16 samples from four Khafre pits and one control) finds halite with gypsum, barite and celestine in the same samples, and bulk sulfur up to 5 percent by weight. Khafre's pits are indeed chloride-rich (chlorine up to 26.7 percent by weight), and the speaker's remark that thorium was very low matches the data, so he was reading a real dataset. But sulfate occurs with the chloride, halite with gypsum is this limestone's normal salt assemblage, and the report makes no statement about acid. For Khufu's pits no analysis was found; the first southern pit was sealed with hard gypsum plaster over a dismantled boat under 41 blocks (secondary accounts), which gives sulfate there a source without acid (`07`, C6, C11, checks 7 to 9; `06`, claim 10). Files `06` and `03` record that no boat-pit chemistry was found; `07`'s second pass read the ISIDA report and is followed here.
- **Breaker.** In a conductive half-space a pit does not open a circuit: current flows around and under it. At the product ceiling the acid would conduct about 1 S/m (recalled, order of magnitude), 40 to 100 times damp limestone, so a filled pit would lower the resistance across it; sourced conductivities start above the ceiling strength (`07`, C11; `09`, K17 and row 45).
- **Osiris Shaft.** Surveyed: three levels 9.62, 13.25 and 7.50 m deep; water at 33.45 m below datum in 1997, following the regional water table. The excavation report lists funerary finds only: 26th Dynasty sarcophagi, shabtis, amulets, bones, and 6th Dynasty sherds dating the first cutting. Seyfzadeh and Schoch report reddish to black, possibly vitrified, veins in Level 2 from personal observation and say samples should be tested (`07`, C12, C3).
- **Use of the acid.** Old Kingdom Giza metalwork that has been analysed is arsenical copper, smelted (one abstract, 15 objects, and search summaries). The earliest arguable recipe for a mineral acid is about AD 1300 and the earliest secure sulfuric acid recipe is 16th century AD, a gap of some 3,800 to 4,050 years. No texts, vessels, residues or acid-etched tailings are known; the surviving record cannot prove a negative (`07`, C14, check 14).
- **Wall deposits.** The 1978 Arizona Bureau letter (sample 56411, read as OCR text) reports mostly calcite with some halite and gypsum, no method or quantities. The 2026 study finds the Queen's Chamber efflorescence to be halite: X-ray diffraction on seven samples shows only halite, calcite and quartz, with sulfur by EDX at about 3, 6 and 0.6 percent in an unidentified phase. The chamber was cleaned in 1995 to 1997 without records and the 2026 sampling took only efflorescence and loose material, so a thin bonded sulfate layer is not excluded. The mortar is gypsum plaster that sheds halite, and halite and gypsum are native to Giza limestone, so all three 1978 phases are expected without acid (`06`, claims 1 and 2, checks 1 to 7). For the Gallery, a salt crust was cleaned over 12 months in 1998 (Hawass 2003) with no composition published; the Edgars' 1910 passage-by-passage account confines the incrustation to the Horizontal Passage and Queen's Chamber and does not mention the Gallery (`06`, claim 3).
- **The decisive test does not exist yet.** No sulfur or oxygen isotope data exist for any Giza gypsum (mortar, bedrock or crust); no height-resolved salt map; no published description of pitting or joint leaching in the claimed wetted zone. Petrie and the Edgars describe very fine joints and salt, not pits. In 1910 salt was concentrated in the Queen's Chamber and Horizontal Passage, where the hypothesis puts liquid, and a null model has to explain that; the same account mentions none in the Gallery, which the hypothesis also fills, and Petrie's salt band in the niche is not what a complete fill predicts. Efflorescence grew between 2022 and 2024, a present-day process (`06`, pass/fail criteria, claims 2 and 4, Steelman). On this ground the hypothesis is untested, not refuted.
- **Rate.** Direct lightning strikes on the pyramid come at 0.03 to 0.12 per year from a national-mean flash density, one every 8 to 33 years (`09`, row 41; screening verdict S31 `undefined`). The speaker refers to a plateau-scale storm generator that the transcripts do not describe.

### 10.6 Thesis

What the evidence base supports, stated without going past it:

1. **The Great Pyramid was built in Khufu's reign, bears his name, and was built by an organised workforce housed on site.** Merer's logbook (read at source) records Tura casing stone delivered to Akhet-Khufu under Ankhhaf in a dated year (the meaning of Akhet-Khufu is not in the files; the tie to this pyramid runs through Tallet's casing-volume figure); the work-gang graffiti and the boat-pit cover stones carry Khufu's name (secondary); the mortar dates agree (secondary); Heit el-Ghurab housed the workforce (read at source). The hypothesis neither disputes nor absorbs this.

2. **The structure and its complex carry what the files name as the furnishings of a royal funerary complex:** a lockable stone coffer in the upper chamber, closure devices in the passages, a temple pavement and a valley temple, queens' pyramids, boat burials and cemeteries. In the files this rests on Petrie's survey of the coffer and the passages (read at source) and, for the complex and the comparison with other pyramids, on labels carried from the sources and on secondary pages; the labels are not functions tested in the files. No text or body from this pyramid is in the files, and the file itself says a tomb and a plant are not mutually exclusive. The funerary reading is therefore supported by attribution and comparison, not proven from the chamber.

3. **The evidence base holds no positive evidence of an industrial purpose.** Of the hypothesis's fifteen evidence claims that bear on purpose, those that survive verification are structural facts open to any reading (closed shafts, air flow, a flat top of unknown history, salt on walls), and every trace offered as diagnostic of acid has a documented non-acid source or no analysis. The one pattern the files flag as favourable, salt concentrated in 1910 in the Queen's Chamber and Horizontal Passage where the hypothesis puts liquid, is also inconsistent with the Gallery fill the hypothesis requires, since the same account mentions no salt there. Nothing an acid plant must leave has been found, within the limits of what was searched; the one test that could discriminate, sulfur isotopes on the chamber gypsum against mortar and bedrock, has not been done.

4. **The claimed process cannot have run as described** (sections 2 to 9): it has no design basis; its water has no source at the needed level, no inlet, no head and no pump; its suction step is excluded at every documented water level (none is measured under Khufu itself); its gas-phase catalyst is 13 orders of magnitude short in rate and 7 to 8 orders short in acoustic power; its field cannot be stored in leaky rock; its oxygen supply through a still surface takes years; its product could not exceed about 0.023 mol/L (0.026 at the most favourable corner); and its hydrogen chloride step does not occur at ambient temperature. Each of these rests on sourced values and recomputed arithmetic, and the screening reports both ends of every input range.

The conclusion the evidence carries is therefore: a royal monument of Khufu's funerary complex, by attribution, furnishing and comparison; not a chemical plant, by the absence of every trace a plant must leave and by the physical failure of the process as claimed. What the files cannot settle is listed in section 11.

## 11. What the process would need, and what remains open

### 11.1 Additions the process would need before it could be tested as feasible

Each is unevidenced at the site; most would turn the claim into a different process.

1. A design basis: product, strength, tonnage, cycle time, cycles per year, feed rate, and the strength the named uses require.
2. Water: a surface at or above the inlet (+16.97 m) or a conduit at base level; a head source of 0.80 MPa and a check that the masonry holds it; a moving element, actuator and seal in the Descending Passage; valves seating against 0.8 MPa; a drain with a receiving volume; an outlet from the Queen's Chamber.
3. Feed: a measured hydrogen sulfide flux; the conversion chemistry, temperature and heat source in the satellite pyramids; a delivery shaft and a way to hold the gas in the King's Chamber with open shafts; an exit for the nitrogen.
4. Oxidation: a hot solid catalyst (industry: vanadium pentoxide near 430 °C) or a nitrogen-oxide carrier at a fraction near 1e-4; or, for the wet route, an aeration step raising oxygen transfer 1e3 to 1e4 times above a still surface together with iron(III) near 1e-5 mol/L or manganese(II) near 1e-4 mol/L in the water.
5. Walls: a gypsum coat formed beforehand and shown to survive fresh-water fills; mortar joints shown to hold.
6. Energy, if the acoustic catalyst is kept: a continuous drive above 20 kHz near 8e5 V/m on a block resonance, and a catalytic surface in the antechamber. Lightning supplies neither.
7. Hydrogen chloride: concentration far above the ceiling and strong heating or distillation.

### 11.2 Measurements that would move the evidence either way

Sulfur and oxygen isotopes of chamber gypsum against mortar and bedrock; a section through a Queen's Chamber wall surface with the sulfur phase identified; the composition of the Gallery crust removed in 1998; four-point resistivity along a Giza vein fill; analyses of Khufu's boat-pit deposits and a survey of pit walls; microanalysis of vein and pit samples for glass and spherules; a measured water table under Khufu for any period; a measured hydrogen sulfide flux in plateau caves or groundwater; an acoustic coupling test between the shaft slab and the chamber; an in-situ impulse response of the antechamber; permeability of core stone, lining stone and mortar.
